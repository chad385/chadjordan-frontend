A literal photograph of a real, ordinary scene on 1970s analog film — dark, grainy, realistic; not a concept or a metaphor. THE PALETTE IS THE POINT, and it is COLD: the ambient light and every shadow are a deep, desaturated navy blue — a cold blue-hour / moonlight fill with faint fog in the air — never warm, brown, amber, or sepia. Against that cold navy ground, one small practical source throws a small warm gold pool — a small aperture reads gold, a wide lit facade reads red, so keep the warm area small. The whole frame lives on the tension between the cold navy majority and that one small warm aperture; if the image reads all-warm or amber, it is WRONG — the ground and shadows must stay cold navy. Ordinary spaces and ordinary objects, never aspirational or upmarket — never a grand, pristine, or "successful" room. Light described as physical objects and their glow, never as mood. THE SUBJECT COMES FROM THE ARTICLE: the specific object, place, or moment the piece is really about, shown literally — a different subject for every article, always in this same world and grade. MOSTLY NO PEOPLE: default to person-absent frames — objects, surfaces, and places with evidence that someone has just been there. A figure appears only when the article is genuinely about a person, and then in profile or turned away, never facing the lens. Depth comes from a soft, out-of-focus near-field mass in the foreground — the edge of a table, a chair back, a doorframe — with the subject resolved behind it. VARY THE WORLD FROM ARTICLE TO ARTICLE: a different setting, a different object, and a different warm source each time. The desk-and-lamp scene (a desk lamp over a notebook, papers, or a coffee cup) is the cliché to avoid — use it only if the article is literally about sitting at a desk. Range widely, for example: a stairwell landing, a parking garage, a corridor at night, a conference room after everyone has left, a lobby, a train platform, a kitchenette, a car interior, a storage room, a print room, an empty shopfront, a loading dock, a hotel hallway, an elevator. Warm sources range as widely: a lit doorway down a corridor, a phone screen face-up on a surface, a single lit office across a courtyard, a car's dome light, a stairwell bulb, an under-cabinet light, a vending machine glow, a projector left on, a streetlight through glass. Restrained composition, negative space, deep cold shadow.
