# Competitive landscape — Chad Jordan Studio

The competition is other people who do brand work for firms this size. Website platforms and template builders are not the competition and should not be argued against.

**Who a prospect is also talking to:**
- **Small brand studios.** Good craft, often strong visual work. Usually start from a style and fit the client into it. Strategy is light or borrowed.
- **Freelance designers.** Affordable, fast, often talented. They execute a brief; they don't write it. The firm still has to know what's true about itself, and most don't.
- **Marketing agencies.** Sell campaigns and channels. Brand is treated as a logo refresh on the way to the retainer.
- **Doing it in-house.** A partner writes the copy, someone's nephew does the logo, an AI tool fills the gaps. Fast and cheap, and it reads that way.

**What's different here:**
- **Twenty years of agency-scale creative direction** — national campaigns, Webby-honored platforms, $2B+ brand revenue scale — applied to firms that normally can't access that level of judgment.
- **The excavation method.** Nothing is invented. The brand comes out of what is already true about the firm: find the dissonance, dig for what's true, cut until only that's left, build around it. Competitors tend to add; this work mostly removes.
- **The brand brain.** A written source of truth the whole brand is built from. Most firms, including most that have paid for branding, have never had one.
- **Built to be lived in.** Identity, image world, site, and the system that keeps it running come as one piece, so the team can use it daily. Durindal's team still runs decisions through their filter.

**How to talk about competitors:** never by name, never with contempt. Describe the approach plainly and let the reader see the difference.
