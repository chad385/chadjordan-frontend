# Content hooks — Chad Jordan Studio

Territory the content should come from. Each hook is a starting point for an argument, not a headline to copy.

- **The ninety-second judgment.** Strangers decide about a firm in about ninety seconds, from whatever is in front of them. What they're actually reading, and what it tells them.
- **Why good firms lose to worse-looking ones.** The competitor with the weaker team and the stronger presentation wins the pitch. How that happens and what it costs over a year.
- **What a brand brain is, and why almost nobody has one.** Most firms, even ones that paid for branding, have a logo and a color palette but nothing written down about who they are. What goes in one and what changes once it exists.
- **Excavation.** Positioning built from what's already true holds up in a sales meeting, including the follow-up questions. How to find it.
- **Addition by subtraction.** Most brand work is cutting: borrowed language, extra services, claims nobody believes. Why removing things is harder than adding them.
- **What twenty years inside big-brand work teaches you about small ones.** The habits large brands rely on that small firms skip, and which of them actually matter at a smaller scale.
- **AI slop and brand voice.** AI tools write in an average voice by default. Without a written brand voice, every firm using them starts to sound the same. (Proven territory: the two published brand-voice articles.)
- **Over-explaining and discounting.** The pitch habits that show up when the first impression undersold the firm.

**Retired — do not use:** platform risk, deplatforming, SaaS pricing, website ownership, rent-vs-own economics, moving off Squarespace / Framer / GoHighLevel.
